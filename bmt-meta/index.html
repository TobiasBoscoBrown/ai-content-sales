🎯 The Thesis

The Eden research isn't telling BMT to pivot. It's telling BMT to stop selling the cheapest unit of work (videos per month) and start selling the expensive thing only BMT can deliver end-to-end: an AI-content engine that generates discovery, runs personalized pitches, and reports tracked outcomes.

The current ROADMAP_V2 offer — "30K+ monthly views in 60 days, guaranteed" — is a great wedge offer. It's defensible (Kathy's data backs it). It's measurable. It closes deals. But it's a feature, not a category. Every month BMT runs that offer alone, the price ceiling stays at $1,200/mo because that's the going rate for "videos per month."

The companies winning in 2026 aren't the ones making the best single video. They're the ones testing the most variations fastest, AND closing on the attention they generate.

BMT is the only entity in this conversation that can credibly do both halves of that sentence. The 90+ videos/month and the AI pitch outreach. The variant testing and the tracked CTA. The TOFU and the BOFU. That's the position to claim.

🏰 What BMT Has That No Competitor Has

1. Tracked CTAs at the platform level

Per-platform tracking links with bot-vs-human filtering, geo + device breakdown, click demographics. Most "AI video" tools dump an MP4 and call it done. BMT measures whether the video moved a human. This is the single biggest differentiator and it's already shipped.

2. End-to-end pipeline ownership

Script generation, TTS, codegen scenes, captions, assembly, captions burn-in, scheduling, upload, and analytics — all in one repo. Competitors stitch 6 SaaS tools together and lose data at every seam.

3. Pitch-video personalization at scale

The Quick Pitch Studio scrapes a prospect's website, extracts brand colors and niche vocabulary, picks a voice, writes a personalized hook, and renders a 30-second pitch with their colors and a custom CTA. Nobody listed in the AI SDR section of the research has this. Amplemarket sends emails. 11x.ai sends emails. Artisan sends emails. BMT sends a video addressed by name.

4. Real client outcome data

Kathy Brown: 31,384 YouTube views in 28 days, search-driven, fully attributed. Royal Sparkle Creations Case Study PDF already exists in the repo. This is the proof asset that converts cold traffic.

5. The operator-of-one cost structure

One operator running 388 videos across 5 clients = a margin profile competitors can't match without burning cash on humans. The engine is the leverage. Every new client adds revenue without adding headcount.

💰 The New Offer Stack

The current single-tier "Audience Launch ($1,500) + Audience Growth retainer ($1,200/mo)" structure works for the wedge. But the research suggests three more SKUs that lift the price ceiling and capture different buyer types.

SKU A — AI Pitch Studio (new, productized)

$99/pitch · $499/mo unlimited. One-off or subscription. Founders point us at 5-50 prospects, we ship a personalized pitch video for each, hosted on a /pitch/{slug} share page with tracked CTAs. Buyer = early-stage founders, agency BDs, fundraising teams. Time-to-value: 1 day.

SKU B — Audience Launch + GEO (upgrade)

$2,000 flat · $1,500/mo retainer. The V2 audience growth offer plus the new GEO Layer: 4 comparison articles/month, schema-marked, cross-posted to Reddit/LinkedIn, Eden-researched. Buyer = SMBs who want to be cited by ChatGPT, not just seen on TikTok.

SKU C — Tribe OS (full stack)

$3,500/mo. Audience Launch + GEO + AI Pitch Studio + dedicated dashboard with weekly review. The "AI Marketing OS" pitch from the research, applied. Buyer = $1M+ revenue businesses who want to consolidate 6 tools into one operator. Sell 3 of these and BMT is at $10K MRR with one operator.

The pricing logic

SKU A captures founders who'd never pay $1,500 for content but will pay $99 for a hand-tailored pitch to land Hamel. SKU B raises the ACV of existing buyers by ~$500/mo for ~10% more work. SKU C is the category claim — the only product on the market that does TOFU video + GEO + BOFU pitch in one engine. Even one Tribe OS client funds 3 weeks of operator runway.

⚠️ Risks & Sustainability Filter

Rubber-banding risk

3 SKUs at once is more surface than one operator can cleanly maintain. Do not launch all three in week 1. Sequence: SKU A this week (smallest, productized), SKU B in week 3, SKU C in month 2 once SKU A has at least one paying customer. Each one validates the next.

Engine state check

The pitch video sweep bug we just fixed today shows the engine still has rough edges that will surface under SKU A volume. Before broadcasting SKU A, run 5 internal pitch jobs and watch for failure modes. Add monitoring on output_video_path 404s — that's the new SLA-relevant metric.

What's automatically de-prioritized

Twitch pipeline, mass new-client cold outreach, and any "build a new feature" work that isn't in the 90-day playbook above. The research is clear: winning in 2026 isn't about more features, it's about positioning the features you already shipped at 10x the price.

The thing that could kill this

If Hamel and Cecilia (and the other 18 prospects in the warm list) don't engage with the regenerated pitch videos, the SKU A thesis is wrong and we should kill it before building infrastructure around it. First 7 days of pitch send data is the validation gate. If reply rate <5%, iterate the script not the SKU. If reply rate <1%, pause SKU A and stay focused on V2 audience growth offer.

Bottom-bottom line

BMT didn't need this research to know what to build — it had already built it. What it needed was language and pricing to match what was on the shelf. The Eden playbook is that language. Use it. Update the deck. Send the pitches. See who bites.
